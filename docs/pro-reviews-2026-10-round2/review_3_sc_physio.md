## Headline

The reconstructed and new-level classes are mostly well built. Ladders move one variable at a time, every MAIN skill step lists an easier rung (the gap is in PRACTICE, F26), 29 of the 34 gated rungs meet their own gate before they first appear, and no class exceeds its set cap. The achievability problems sit in three places:

1. **The authentic source blocks.** L2:001-030 (#101-130) and L3:001-020 (#201-220) ask for much more than the classes before them build. Examples: L-sit pull-ups at #103, clap push-ups at #103, a high-bar pullover at #123, 10-15 negative muscle-ups per class at #202-206, and full single-leg squats at #209. 373 of the 400 authentic MAIN steps carry no step-specific easier rung. The surrounding classes, mainly L1:069-099 and L2:066-098, have to do the preparing.
2. **The end-stage shapes run ahead of their own gates.** Full flag, full front lever, full back lever and straddle planche all appear before their gates, the full flag only 3 classes after the first-ever straddle flag.
3. **Recovery thins out late in the programme.** Deload gaps reach 65-72 classes at each level boundary from L4 on. L5-L6 have weeks with 4-5 RPE-8 days. In L6-L7 the weekly layout puts three heavy elbow-flexor days in a row.

On timelines for an ordinary adult: the late rungs are compressed. The straddle front lever comes at about month 17 and full-lever holds at about month 21. The full flag comes about 100 classes after the first tuck flag. The assisted-to-negative one-arm step takes 5 weeks. With the same-class fallbacks this is safe once gate order and spacing are fixed, but most ordinary adults will finish L7 still on the straddle or advanced-tuck rung of those skills. That is acceptable only because the library calls those shapes ceilings, not targets.
