# Calibration review: is Street Gymnastics too easy or too slow for its audience?

Reviewer lens: an independent calisthenics coach who designs for app audiences. The typical user is a healthy, somewhat active adult who can do some push-ups and 0–3 pull-ups and wants visible progress. Earlier rounds asked "is every step achievable and safe?". This review asks the opposite question. It keeps tendon-paced rung spacing for levers, planche, flag and one-arm work, keeps every "Start when" gate ahead of its rung, and keeps all recovery days.

The data comes from `classes_digest.txt` (read in full) and `curriculum_v2.json` (stated minutes and set counts). The scripts are `calib/analyze.py` and `calib/digest_stats.py`, and the numbers are in `calib/digest_stats.txt`. Original-source classes (L1:001–040, L2:041–070, L3:001–020) are only used as context.

In weeks 1–3 of the original-source block (shrugs, front raises, grip switches and squats) there is no push-up at all, which is too easy for this user. That block belongs to the other agent's revision, which should put push-ups and rows into week 1.

**Session time.** Stated lengths average 28–52 minutes against the 55-minute cap. In L1, the harder classes run 29–37 minutes but already use 14 of 14 sets, and L4 classes average 17.8 of 18. So the room is in time, not in sets. The fixes below therefore raise reps, holds and rungs, and swap filler sets for useful ones, without adding sets.

## 3. Leave alone: these are rightly challenging or rightly paced

- **Front and back lever ladders.** Front lever: tuck at L2:075, advanced tuck at L3:035, one-leg at L4:092, straddle at L6:024, full at L7:030. The back lever follows the same pattern. Each rung waits for its gate plus its time in weeks, and holds grow within a rung (5 → 12 s). This is tendon-correct, and already hard for this user.
- **Planche.** Lean, then tuck (L4:017), advanced tuck (L5:017) and the straddle check (L7:043). Slow, and right for wrists and elbows. Only the hold-time dip in C12 needs fixing.
- **Flag spacing.** Tuck flag at L5:073, straddle flag at L6:072, full flag at L7:041. Also the **one-arm chain**: archer at L4:062, typewriter at L7:021, assisted one-arm at L7:063, negative at L7:084.
- **Dragon flag chain.** Bent-knee negative at L3:026, one-leg negative at L4:009, full negative at L5:002, dragon flag at L5:058, sets of 4 in L7. It is challenging and well gated.
- **L2 main block.** Pull-ups 3x4 → 3x8–9–10 (L2:098), dips 3x6 → 2x10, push-ups 3x10–14, the assisted muscle-up rungs (jump, band, negative) and the chest-to-bar entry.
- **L3 basics volume.** Pullup 4x7–2x10, dips 3x12–14, push-ups 2x18, squat 3x28, and the readiness-check block at L3:065–069. This is the level the rest of the program should be measured against (see C3).
- **RPE-8 placement in L5–L7.** About two RPE-8 days a week on lever and muscle-up days (for example L5:015, 019, 043, 047; L7:009, 012, 016, 019). L7's 3:1 deload rhythm (weeks 4, 8, 12) and one RPE 2–3 recovery day a week also stay.
- **L7 as a whole.** Brief full-lever holds with the straddle in the same class, muscle-ups at 3x4, deficit and freestanding HSPU negatives, and the benchmark week L7:092–098. It is dense and demanding; leave it alone.
- **Day-4 recovery** in every level, and **the deload weeks in L4, L5 and L7**. These are safety-critical, and C7 and C8 keep them.
