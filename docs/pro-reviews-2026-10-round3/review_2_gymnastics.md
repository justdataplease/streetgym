# Street Gymnastics: skill-progression review (round 3, reviewer 2: gymnastics / hand balancing)

Scope: all 700 classes in `classes_digest.txt` and the movement library. I traced every movement with scripts in `r2\` (`trace.py` first/last use, doses and gaps; `wrist.py` wrist, overhead and straight-arm load by day; `caps.py` and `permov.py` set caps; `flfix.py` top rung versus fallback per class; `l7swap.py` simulates the Level 7 reorder). "Heavy wrist day" means at least 2 sets of planche-family work or at least 2 sets of overhead pressing. Class numbers convert to months at about 30 classes a month.

**The whole journey.** First appearances, compared with what I see in consistent adult beginners:
- strict MU L3:077 (~9 months; typical 9–18)
- freestanding attempts L5:071 (~15.5 months; a hold by 20–23 months is typical)
- tuck / advanced tuck / one-leg / straddle / full FL: 5.8 / 7.7 / 12.9 / 17.2 / 20.7 months (typical full FL 3–5+ years)
- tuck / advanced tuck / one-leg / straddle / full BL: 7.5 / 9.3 / 13.3 / 17.3 / 20.8 months (typical full BL 1–2.5 years, so on pace)
- tuck / straddle / full flag: 15.6 / 18.8 / 21.1 months
- tuck / advanced-tuck planche: 10.4 / 13.7 months
- dragon flag ~15 months; wall HSPU ~16.7 months; freestanding HSPU negative ~21.8 months

By Level 7 a typical adult will have:
- a freestanding handstand
- a strict muscle-up for low reps (some women will stay on band MU)
- a back lever at one-leg or straddle, often a brief full BL
- dragon-flag negatives or reps
- wall HSPU
- front lever at advanced tuck or one-leg
- tuck or advanced-tuck planche
- tuck or straddle flag

That plateau is acceptable only because every Level 7 top rung has its straddle or assisted rung in the same class. That holds for every skill except the front lever in L6–L7 (F6, the plateau rung is missing) and the original-source blocks (F1–F4, no easier rung at all). With those fixed, the plateaus are safe and do not need extra wording.

## 3. Keep (do not change)

- **Gate discipline.** Almost every "Start when" gate is prescribed before its rung:
  - strict MU: pull-ups 2x10 (L3:063), chest-to-bar 3x5 (L3:060), straight-bar dips 3x10 (L3:036), then L3:065
  - advanced-tuck FL: 3x10s tuck holds (L3:027, L3:034), then L3:035
  - one-leg FL: advanced tuck 3x10s (L4:085), then L4:092, 157 classes after the first advanced tuck
  - toe pulls: 3x40s chest-to-wall (L4:036–069), then L4:071
  - kick-ups: 2x45s (L4:071–100), then L5:036
  - wall HSPU: negatives 3x5 (L5:090), then L6:007
  - the flag, dragon flag and one-arm pull-up ladders are gated the same way
- **The handstand build.** The handstand is practised before the main block on every training day. The cartwheel exit (L4:036) comes 35 classes before toe pulls; the box pike comes 100 classes before the wall; chest-to-wall builds to 2x45s before kick-ups; balance windows run daily from L5:071. Inversion always comes before pressing in the same class.
- **Readiness checks as single sets** with the easier rung in the same class (L3:065, L3:068–069, L6:078–083, L7:092–098). The L7 last rungs sit beside their straddle or assisted rung (full_front + straddle_front, full_back + straddle_back, full_flag + straddle_flag, straddle_planche + advanced-tuck planche, freestanding HSPU negative + deficit).
- **Weekly rhythm.** Day-4 and day-7 recovery and a deload every fourth week in L4–L7. Wrist-heavy days in L4–L6 never run three in a row (one exception, L6:097).
- **Back-lever and dragon-flag pacing**, including the gate "the elbows and biceps feel nothing the next day" before advanced-tuck back lever, and the weekly German hang.
