The class where each one comes, and roughly when at the pace you picked.

  17. L10:100The Level 10 test checks every skill family at its top rung. By now every skill holds Master, and every Elite is at least 70% of the way.

Every skill, every belt

A belt is its target in good form: 10 sets for Beginner, 15 for Intermediate, 20 for Advanced, 25 for Master and 30 for Elite. A set on a harder rung counts for the easier rungs of its ladder too, at the belt it reaches: a 6 s full planche is an Elite set of the straddle planche. A green outline is a belt the classes give: the class where it lands, its target and when. A dashed one comes through a harder rung of the same ladder: a belt held on the full back lever is held on the straddle and tuck back levers too, never higher. "first" is the class where you first do a set at that belt's target. The classes take every skill to Master. A grey cell is an Elite the classes take most of the way, the top rung of a ladder: its percentage is the sets they give at the target out of the 30 it needs, and a repeated block or your own training does the rest.

Read it honestly

  • These are working-set targets, done with a rep or two still in you, not single maximal efforts. Elite is that target thirty times over.
  • The full planche and the one-arm pull-up come at about the research medians for committed, lighter athletes. Most people take longer: expect block repeats there.
  • Heavier, taller, female and over-40 athletes typically take 20–50% longer on the levers, flag, planche, muscle-up and one-arm work. The handstand, the core and the legs change much less.
  • The last of the 40 first belts lands at L9:095.
